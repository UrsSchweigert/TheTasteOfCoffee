# USP iterations

The USP went through three rounds; each was tested against the personas and the weakest point fixed in the next.

## Round 1: "Scan, brew, rate: your coffee journal."

- Works for Jonas. Gives Mira, Tomás and Selin no reason to pay.
- Weakness: a journal is a feature, not a difference. Nothing here explains the subscription.

## Round 2: "Coffee ratings you can actually compare, because nobody types."

- Names the real difference: fixed criteria and fixed brew parameters make every entry comparable.
- Weakness: comparability only helps people who see other people's data. Free users log into a void, and the paid tier is empty until many free users log.

## Round 3 (final): "Know how a bean tastes and how to brew it before you open the bag."

- Free: scan a bag and log a brew in under 30 seconds, all taps, no typing. Your own history and taste profile are yours for free.
- Paid: see what all structured brews say about a bean: its score profile, which method suits it, and the recipes that scored highest.
- Fix for the void: **give-to-get**. A free user sees the community's overall score for any bean they have rated themselves. Everything deeper is paid.

## Supporting claims, in order of strength

1. **Comparable by design.** Eight fixed criteria and a closed flavour vocabulary. No reviews to read, only data to compare.
2. **Recipe-level insight.** Ratings are tied to method, grind, ratio and temperature. The answer is "this bean, as V60 at 1:16, scores 8.4", not "4 stars".
3. **Private by default.** Only aggregates are shown, never an individual's log, and only once at least 5 people have rated a bean.
4. **Language-free.** Every label is a translated key, so the data set is global from day one.
