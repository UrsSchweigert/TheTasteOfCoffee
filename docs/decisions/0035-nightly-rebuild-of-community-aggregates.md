# 0035 Community aggregates are rebuilt every night from the source of truth

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-11-26
- Decided by: owner for the four points of Decision 1 (2026-11-26); lead (ADR 0014) for everything else. The Scan cost of about 3 USD a month at 50,000 users is accepted by the owner; no fixed-fee resource is added.

## Context

docs/05 (Aggregation pipeline), D1, D2 and ADRs 0010, 0012 and 0017 planned an incremental aggregator: a Lambda on the table's stream adds each brew to running totals, applies differences on edits and deletes, subtracts a deleted account's brews from the stream's old image, keeps one `RATER#` marker per rater, and counts a brew only if its author had consent when it was written. Nothing of this is built. Checked against the code on 2026-11-26: `backend/` and `infra/` have no scheduled Lambda, no Scheduler schedule and no `Scan` in production code (the one `ScanCommand` is in a test); `backend/src/repository/keys.ts` has the unused builders `aggregateAllKey`, `aggregateMethodKey`, `raterMarkerKey` and `rankingKey`; the stream is enabled (`NEW_AND_OLD_IMAGES`, M1-T1); the boundary policy allows `dynamodb:Query`, `GetItem`, writes and `ExportTableToPointInTime` but not `dynamodb:Scan`.

The incremental design has to stay correct under every kind of change at once: create, edit, delete, a brew moved to another shelf entry, consent changes, account deletion, TTL deletes, retried stream batches. Each of these is a place for a counter to drift, and a drifted counter cannot be found, because the source of truth is never read again. It also cannot judge a brew by what is known later (plausibility, a user's whole history), and withdrawing consent would need a pass over all of the user's brews anyway.

On 2026-11-26 the owner decided to rebuild from scratch every night instead, and decided the points of Decision 1.

## Decision

1. **Owner decisions of 2026-11-26.**
   - **Nightly Scan.** One scheduled job Scans the table and rebuilds all aggregates and rankings from nothing. The stream aggregator is not built.
   - **Withdrawal removes contributions.** Withdrawing consent is one write of the profile (`consent.contribute_to_aggregates`, the existing `PUT /profile`) and takes effect at the next successful nightly run. It removes the user's ratings from the community numbers only. The personal log is untouched: no brew, shelf entry or statistic of the user changes. Re-consent is the same single write; from the next successful run it counts all of the user's rated brews again, including those made while consent was withdrawn, because the rebuild looks at the profile as it is at run time.
   - **Daily one-vote weights.** Every cup counts. A user's plausible cups of one bean on one UTC day share one vote, each cup weighing 1/n. Distinct raters count users, never weights.
   - **Cap 10.** At most 10 cups of one bean per user per UTC day count; the rest are ignored.
2. **What this replaces.** ADR 0010 stays for the key formats (`AGG#ALL`, `AGG#METHOD#<method>`, `RANK#<facet>` with `<period>` the calendar day of the run, `BREW#` and `BREWID#`) and for the rule that brews are listed by `brewedAt`. This ADR replaces its aggregator consequences (ignore `BREW_REF`, treat a moved brew as remove plus insert) and ADR 0012's (the aggregator ignores `SHELF` items): nothing reads the stream. It replaces, in ADR 0017 and docs/07, the D1 rule "counts a brew only if its author had consent when it was written" with "counts a user's brews while the profile says consent at run time", and the D2 mechanism "subtract, then delete" with "delete; the next run no longer counts the brews". Both stay decided by the lead (ADR 0014). The sentence "counts buy-again only for ratings that carry it" (ADR 0026) still holds. docs/05, docs/07 and docs/04 are updated in M3-T12, not here.
3. **Weights, exactly.**
   - Scores are integer half-points (1 to 10 in half steps, so 2 to 20).
   - A counted cup in a group of n counted cups has weight `L / n`, where `L = lcm(1..10) = 2520` is the fixed common denominator. Because n is at most 10 (the cap), every weight is an integer and every sum is an exact integer. A group's cups together weigh exactly `L`: one vote.
   - **Bean level:** groups are (user, bean, UTC day of `brewedAt`), n is that user's counted cups of that bean that day. **Method level:** groups are (user, bean, method, UTC day), n is the counted cups of that bean and method that day. A user can thus move a bean's overall mean by at most one vote a day, and a method's mean by at most one vote a day. The cap is applied before n is computed.
   - **Items keep integers only.** Per criterion: the sum of weight x half-points, the sum of weight x half-points squared (for the spread), and the weighted count (the weights of cups that scored it). The eight criteria are all required on a rated brew (`Scores`, ADR 0026), so each criterion's weighted count is `L x votes` and the mean `sum / weighted count` equals `sum / (L x votes)`. The weighted mean is `sum / weighted count`; the spread is the standard deviation in points on the 0 to 10 scale. With W the weighted count, S the sum and SS the sum of squares (half-points), the variance in half-points squared is exactly `(W x SS - S^2) / W^2`, computed in BigInt or as a rational and rounded only at the end (W x SS passes 2^53 at a few thousand votes, and the float form `SS/W - mean^2` cancels). The spread is the square root, divided by 2 for points, rounded to one decimal at the end. Mean and spread are computed only when read, from integers, and never stored. Also kept: counts for intensities, notes, buy-again (only cups that carry it, ADR 0026) and recipe histogram buckets, weighted the same way.
   - **A distinct-rater count per part.** Buy-again, each intensity dimension, the flavour notes and each recipe parameter histogram are optional on a brew, so the users who contributed to them can be fewer than the users who rated the bean. Each of these parts keeps its own distinct-rater count (users with at least one counted cup that carries that part), next to the item's overall count. The eight criteria need none of their own: every counted cup has all eight.
   - **Bound and assertion.** One cup adds at most 20 x 2520 = 50,400 to a criterion's sum and at most 400 x 2520 = 1,008,000 to its sum of squares. The general exactness bound is 2^53 divided by 1,008,000, about 8.9e9 counted cups (about 1.8e11 for the sums alone). The test size (at most 10 counted cups per user-bean-day, 200 users, 20,000 brews) stays far below it. The code asserts `Number.isSafeInteger` after every addition and fails the whole run on overflow instead of losing precision.
   - **Distinct raters** are the number of distinct users with at least one counted cup for that item. The item stores the number, never a list, and never a user id.
4. **Plausibility rules, in a fixed order.** A brew is judged by the first rule that rejects it:
   1. not consented (no profile and `false` both mean no consent),
   2. unrated,
   3. brewed in the future: `brewedAt` more than 5 minutes after the run time (clock skew),
   4. brewed before the roast date: the brew's UTC day is earlier than the shelf entry's roast date (the same day counts),
   5. burst: more than 5 brews of one user within any 60 seconds (a sliding window; the earliest 5 count),
   6. bag dose over the bag weight: the running dose of one shelf entry's brews, oldest first, may not exceed the bag size x 1.10; a shelf entry without a known bag size is not judged by this rule,
   7. straight-lining: a user with at least 10 rated brews whose eight scores are the same one value in every brew; all of that user's brews are rejected,
   8. over the daily cap: the first 10 cups of a user-bean-day by `brewedAt`, then brew id, count.

   These are starting values. All of them sit in one constant set (`backend/src/aggregation/config.ts`, M3-T3, with `L`, the cap and the 5-rater minimum), and the owner tunes them from the per-reason counts in the logs (M3-T15); changing one is a code change and a deploy, not a new ADR, unless the rule itself changes. The reason ids are a closed list in `contracts/` (M3-T2). A rejected brew is left out of the numbers and is never deleted, edited or flagged on the user's item: the user's own log does not change. There is no human review queue.
5. **Staleness.** Community numbers are up to about a day old, longer when a run fails (point 7a). The user's own numbers are computed on the device and are not affected. A new brew, an edit, a deletion, a consent change or an account deletion shows in the community numbers after the next successful run, and in no other way.
6. **The job.** One Lambda `cupmark-<stage>-nightly-aggregates` on an EventBridge Scheduler schedule (M3-T9) pages through the table with `Scan` (a fixed page size, eventually consistent reads), reads brews, shelf entries, profiles and bean `META` items, skips every other item type by key prefix, rebuilds everything in memory with a pure function (the run time is read once and passed in), then writes the result. Bean-level facets for rankings: `all`, method, process, roast level, price band and origin. The nightly job and the rebuild core never read the clock more than once.
7. **Fail closed.** A scan that has a page error, a throttle that exhausts its retries or an item that cannot be decoded throws, and the run fails with nothing written. A failure of the rebuild (including the integer assertion) also fails the run before any write. The previous night's items stay as they were, one day older. There is never a partial scan result, so a partial result is never turned into numbers.
7a. **Fail closed for withdrawal and deletion too.** A pipeline that keeps failing must not keep showing a withdrawn or deleted user's ratings. Every aggregate and ranking item is stamped with the run day, and `readAggregate` answers "not available" for an item that is more than 3 days old, defined exactly as (UTC day of the read) - (stamp day) > `MAX_AGE_DAYS` (3, a named constant in the config), the stamp being the UTC day of the run's single run-time read. With the last success on day D, a read on D+3 is still served and a read on D+4 is "not available". So after a few failed nights community numbers disappear instead of going on showing contributions the user took back.
8. **The write, and the window around it.** The result is written with `BatchWriteItem`, then aggregate and ranking items the new set no longer contains are deleted, so a bean whose raters all left disappears (their last contributions are removed, which is the point of D1 and D2). Changed-only writes apply to the `AGG#` items: the job compares the new aggregate items with those the Scan already read and writes the new or different ones. An unchanged `AGG#` item keeps its old stamp and is rewritten only to refresh the stamp once it is 2 or more days behind the run day, so it never reaches the age limit of point 7a while runs succeed. Ranking items are keyed by the run day (ADR 0010), so every ranking item is new each night and yesterday's are deleted: ranking writes plus deletes are a nightly cost bounded by ranked beans x facets, and neither the changed-only rule nor the 2-day refresh applies to them. Writes are idempotent: a second run on the same day with the same input leaves the table unchanged. The write is not one transaction (a table-wide set exceeds the 100-item transaction limit). If the write fails part way, the run fails and the next night repairs it. Until then a reader sees a mix of the old and the new set. This is safe because every item is self-consistent (it is one whole item) and every read goes through the threshold of point 10 item by item. The 5-rater rule is not affected. The promise to a user who withdrew is met at the next successful run, and by the age limit of point 7a if runs keep failing; it is not met inside the window itself.
9. **Cost and size.**
   - **Cost.** The owner has accepted the nightly Scan (decision of 2026-11-26): about 3 USD a month at 50,000 users, an order-of-magnitude figure the lead did not re-derive (docs/06 says to re-run the Pricing Calculator). The Scan reads what the table has; the rebuild adds Lambda compute, not read cost. The rewrite adds one write per changed aggregate or ranking item per night (point 8), which grows with the number of beans and methods and not with the number of brews. The reads of M4 stay one or two item reads per page (docs/05). No resource with a fixed monthly fee is added (invariant 5): a Lambda, a schedule, a log group; no provisioned concurrency, no VPC, no NAT gateway. No custom CloudWatch metrics (they bill per metric): the job's numbers are one structured log line.
   - **Size bound.** One invocation has a timeout of 300 seconds and 512 MB (stage constants in `infra/src/config.ts`, set from the measurement of M3-T10). The job scans one table in one invocation, keeping a compact record per counted-candidate brew in memory. This is meant for the early stage; the cost figure above is the read cost at 50,000 users, and the run time reaches its limit before that (see the scale trigger).
   - **Scale trigger.** When a run on the real table needs more than half of the timeout (150 seconds), or its peak heap exceeds 60% of the memory, a new ADR splits the scan into parallel segments (`Segment` and `TotalSegments`) with a merge step. The duration and the share of the timeout are in the run's log line (M3-T8) and measured before any deploy (M3-T10), so the owner sees it coming; nothing is split on a guess.
   - **Measured (M3-T10, in-memory table, Linux sandbox, 2026-11-27).** The replay test (`npm run test:replay` in `backend/`) builds a seeded history of 20,000 created brews of 200 users and 300 beans (with edits, deletes, consent flips and every plausibility rule) and runs the real handler on it: 20,765 items scanned in 42 pages of 500, 19,599 brews seen, 11,527 counted, 2,261 items written. The run took about 1.0 to 1.3 seconds in the test (0.4% of the 300-second timeout; the limit of this trigger is 150 s). The peak heap was about 142 MB, which is V8 `heapUsed` of the whole test process, sampled after every client request and around the rebuild: it is conservative because it includes the in-memory table's own items (about 77 MB before the run started). The Lambda's 512 MB is the memory of the whole process, so the heap figure is not comparable one to one: in M3-T15 the owner compares the Lambda REPORT line's "Max Memory Used" with 60% of 512 MB. The handler's own log line carries the same count keys as the test's `REPLAY_HANDLER_LOG` line (duration, items scanned, written, deleted, reasons) but no heap key. The test fails above a 60-second budget, above half of the timeout and above 60% of the heap. The in-memory table has no network latency, so the real duration is higher: about 42 Scan round trips plus about 91 batch writes of 25, likely seconds rather than 1.3 s. Extrapolation, not a measurement: the table here is about 21,000 items, and a run is expected to reach the trigger only at a much larger table; the first real run's log line (M3-T15) replaces this guess.
10. **One read path, and the 5-rater threshold (invariant 4).** In `backend/src/repository/`, `readAggregate` (M3-T7) is the only function that returns aggregate data. It returns "not enough raters" when the distinct-rater count of the item is below 5, and then never returns counts or sums. It also withholds each optional part (buy-again, each intensity dimension, the notes, each recipe histogram) whose own distinct-rater count is below 5, so one user cannot be read out of a part of a bean with five raters. It returns "not available" for an item older than `MAX_AGE_DAYS` (point 7a). M4 serves rounded means, spreads and shares only, never raw sums or weighted counts (they would reveal how many cups a user logged on a day). One user with many days can still weigh heavily on a bean with exactly 5 raters, and differencing a bean's `all` item against a facet with overlapping raters can narrow down one user's contribution; rounded outputs and unserved raw counts reduce this, and both are inherent to a threshold of 5 users. The rankings contain only beans with at least 5 distinct raters in that facet's own count (a bean in the price band ranking needs 5 raters whose counted cups are in that band). Beans below the minimum still get their aggregate item, so M4 can answer "not enough raters yet" without a second read path; the item is stored, not served. No route returns aggregates in M3; M4's paid routes use only this function. A structural test fails if another file mentions the aggregate or ranking key builders.
11. **Reference recipes are never read (ADR 0034).** Nothing under `backend/src/aggregation/`, the job handler or the aggregate read path imports or reads `contracts/reference/`. Reference recipes are numbers from published sources, are never counted as raters, and are shown beside community data, never inside it. A structural test pins this.
12. **The stream stays enabled and unused.** M1-T1 pins it and infra tests assert it. With no consumer it costs nothing and nothing can drift. Switching it off is a change to the table that the owner may propose later; it is not part of M3.
13. **Observability.** Per run one log line with fixed keys: duration, items scanned, brews seen, brews counted, one count for every reason id (zeros included), beans with aggregates, beans ranked, share of the timeout used. No user id, bean id or text. Logs only for the numbers; failures also raise an alarm (Addendum 2026-11-27, which supersedes the earlier "no alarm in M3").
14. **Decisions on the documents' disagreements with the code** (the docs change in M3-T12):
    - **Price band ranking.** A price band belongs to a shelf entry (a purchase), not to a bean, so a bean has no single band. The price band facet is built from counted cups whose shelf entry has that band, with its own weight groups (user, bean, band, day) so each band gets a full vote per user and day, and a bean ranks in a band when that band has at least 5 distinct raters of it. A bean can appear in several bands. A shelf entry without a band adds to no band ranking. `keys.ts` already has the `price_band` facet.
    - **Origin ranking.** docs/04 F4 lists origin, `keys.ts` does not have it (ADR 0010 said "joins when vocabulary v2 has the list"). The facet is added as `RANK#ORIGIN#<id>` in M3-T7, ranked by the country: a bean whose origin is a region is ranked under its country (`country` in the vocabulary); `blend`, `other` and `unknown` have no ranking.
    - **Verified attributes only.** An unverified bean carries the attributes picked by the user who created it. The origin, process and roast level facets rank only beans whose attributes are operator-verified (the operator's value is used), so one user's pick cannot place a bean in a list. `unknown` and `other` get no ranking. The `all`, method and price band facets need no bean attribute.
    - **Rankings keep one period.** The sort key is the day of the run (ADR 0010), and the replace deletes older ranking items. Only the last run's lists exist; the history is in the exports.
    - **Top-scoring quarter of brews per method (docs/04 F4, "top recipes").** Deferred to M4. M3 keeps, per method, bucket counts of the recipe parameters (ratio, grind, temperature, time; buckets in M3-T2) over all counted cups. "The top quarter" needs the cups' order by score, which bucket counts cannot give, and the parameter ranges need a design (weights, ties, how few cups are enough) that belongs with the Explore screens. Because the whole table is rebuilt every night, M4 can add another histogram (for example of the cups at or above a score threshold) without migrating anything.
    - **Mean and spread.** docs/05 and F4 ask for mean and spread. The items keep the sum of squares (point 3) so M4 can compute both exactly. M3-T12 updates docs/05 (data model) accordingly.
    - **Give-to-get (docs/04 F4, the rater marker).** The `RATER#` marker is gone, so the mechanism is gone with it. M4 decides give-to-get from the user's own shelf or brews, which needs no per-bean rater list.
    - **docs/06 write estimate ("a brew causes about 3 table writes including aggregates").** With this design a brew causes no aggregate writes. A brew write is a transaction of the brew, its pointer and the shelf entry count (ADR 0010, ADR 0012: about four write units); the nightly rewrite is separate and bounded by the number of aggregate items. M3-T12 corrects the sentence in docs/06 and recomputes nothing else.
    - **"One API function plus three small workers" (docs/05, Components).** Stays true: the workers are the health check, the Apple identity provider function and the nightly aggregates job (the stream aggregator is not built, so it is not a fourth). M3-T12 names them.

## Alternatives

- **The stream aggregator with difference-apply and rater markers (the earlier plan).** Rejected: it has to be right for every kind of change, a lost or doubled batch drifts the totals with no way to detect it, `RATER#` markers cost a write and an item per rater and bean, consent and plausibility cannot be judged from a single record, and deletes depend on the old image being complete. It costs more code and more tests for a smaller guarantee.
- **Per-user recompute on consent change.** Rejected: on withdrawal it would have to find and subtract all of a user's brews from every bean and method they touched (and re-add them on re-consent), with the same drift risk, to get something the nightly rebuild gives for free. A consent change is one profile write; the cost of waiting up to a day is accepted by the owner.
- **Live consent check at read time.** Rejected: it needs the rater list per bean, which the privacy design avoids (the item holds a number only).
- **One rating per user, bean and day (a simple dedupe).** Rejected by the owner: every cup counts; the weights keep the one-vote limit without throwing cups away.
- **Floating-point or fractional weights.** Rejected: sums would differ with the order of addition. Integers with `L = 2520` are exact and checked.
- **Scan in parallel segments from the start.** Not now: more moving parts (a merge step, partial failure across segments) before a measurement shows they are needed. The trigger above says when.
- **Switching the stream off now.** Not part of this change; the owner may propose it later.

## Consequences

- Community numbers are up to a day old and the app says so (M4). Withdrawal, deletion and edits converge on their own, with no bookkeeping that can fail.
- Fail-closed means a broken night leaves yesterday's numbers (visible in the failed Lambda run and the logs), not wrong numbers, and after 3 days nothing is served. A failed or missing run raises an alarm (Addendum 2026-11-27, owner decision, which supersedes the earlier "no alarm in M3").
- The Scan reads the whole table every night, including personal items the job does not use. The cost is accepted; the scale trigger covers growth.
- The job needs `dynamodb:Scan` on the table, which the boundary policy lacks. M3-T9 adds it and the owner re-applies the guardrails before the deploy in M3-T14 (a human step, invariant 9 and the stop rules in CLAUDE.md).
- The unused rater-marker key builder is removed in M3-T7; the table's stream and its output remain.
- When one run no longer fits half of its timeout or the heap bound, a new ADR splits the scan; until then no segmentation code exists.
- Thresholds are tuned from per-reason counts on real data (M3-T15), so the starting values here are expected to change in the config, not in this ADR.

## Addendum 2026-11-27: a failure alarm (owner decision)

The owner decided on 2026-11-27 that a failed nightly run must notify them (answering the lead's offer of one alarm on the job's error metric). This supersedes the "logs only" sentence in point 13 and the "no alarm in M3" sentence in Consequences. Everything else in this ADR stands.

1. **Two CloudWatch alarms per stage**, both on the Lambda `cupmark-<stage>-nightly-aggregates` and both with a name starting `cupmark-<stage>-` and the tag `project=cupmark`:
   - **Errors:** metric `Errors` (namespace `AWS/Lambda`, statistic Sum), threshold `>= 1` over a period of 1 day (86,400 seconds), 1 evaluation period. It fires when a run fails (point 7).
   - **Missing run:** metric `Invocations` (statistic Sum), threshold `< 1` over a period slightly over a day (for example 25 hours, 90,000 seconds; the exact value is a named stage constant), treat missing data as breaching. It fires when the schedule did not run at all (the schedule disabled or broken, or the function gone). On `prod` the schedule is created disabled until the owner enables it (M3-T9), so this alarm is created on `prod` only together with the enabled schedule (a stage flag), to avoid a standing false alarm.
2. **One SNS topic `cupmark-<stage>-alerts`.** Both alarms send their ALARM state to it. The topic policy lets only CloudWatch alarms of this account publish to it (`cloudwatch.amazonaws.com`, conditioned on the alarm ARNs `cupmark-*` of this account) and nothing else.
3. **No subscription in code.** The stack creates no `AWS::SNS::Subscription`: no email address, phone number or other endpoint is in the repository (invariant 8). The owner subscribes their own address once, after the deploy, as a human step (M3-T14), and confirms the subscription mail; M3-T15 checks that a test alert arrives.
4. **Cost and invariant 5.** An alarm costs about 0.10 USD per month, so two alarms are about 0.20 USD per stage per month. An SNS topic and a few email notifications cost nothing at this volume. This is an estimate to verify with the AWS price list before the deploy; it is not a measured figure. The alarms are a small, fixed monthly fee, and invariant 5 requires an approved ADR for that: this addendum is that approval, given by the owner on 2026-11-27, and it covers exactly these two alarms and this topic per stage. Any further alarm is a separate decision (M5 keeps the other alarms and the budgets).
5. **Still no custom metrics.** Both alarms use the built-in Lambda metrics; the run's numbers stay in the log line of point 13.
6. **Permissions.** The Lambda role needs nothing new. The CloudFormation execution policy already allows `cloudwatch:*` and `sns:*` on `cupmark-*` alarm and topic ARNs only, and the boundary policy for runtime roles is unchanged except for statements added later by M3-T9 and M3-T11: `NightlyAggregatesScan` (`dynamodb:Scan` on `table/cupmark-*-main`), `NightlySchedulerInvokesCupmarkFunctions` (`lambda:InvokeFunction` on `function:cupmark-*`) and, for the weekly export, `WeeklyTableExportWrites`.

## Addendum 2 2026-11-27: alarms only from more than 100 users

The owner decided on 2026-11-27 that the failure alarm is wanted only once the app has more than 100 users; before that no alarm is needed and none should cost anything. The design is the lead's (ADR 0014). It amends points 1, 3 and 4 of the first addendum.

1. **A stage flag gates the alarms.** `infra/src/config.ts` has `failureAlarms: { enabled: boolean }` per stage, `false` for `dev` and `prod`. With `false` the stack creates neither alarm and not the topic `cupmark-<stage>-alerts`: no resource and no cost. With `true` it creates both alarms and the topic exactly as the first addendum specifies. This amends point 1 (the alarms are built but gated; the existing `prod` rule that the missing-run alarm exists only while the schedule is enabled still applies when the flag is on).
2. **Subscription (amends point 3).** There is still no subscription in code. The owner's subscribe-and-confirm step and the test alert happen only after the owner has turned the flag on and deployed.
3. **Cost (amends point 4).** 0 USD until the flag is on; about 0.20 USD per stage per month afterwards (estimate, as before). The owner's approval under invariant 5 covers the flag being turned on without a new ADR; it is a one-line change in a pull request.
4. **When to turn it on.** The owner turns the flag on in a pull request when the user count passes 100, then deploys, subscribes their email and tests. To know when that is, the nightly run's log line (point 13) gains one number, `users_seen`: the count of distinct users with at least one brew in the scan. It is a count only, never ids. The Cognito user pool in the console also shows the number of users. M6's launch checklist asks for the decision before launch.


## Addendum 3 2026-11-27: scheduler role trust is account-only

The first dev deploy of `cupmark-dev-core` was refused: both `AWS::Scheduler::Schedule` resources failed with "The execution role you provide must allow AWS EventBridge Scheduler to assume the role." The trust policies of the two scheduler roles (nightly and weekly export) carried `aws:SourceArn` (the exact schedule ARN) next to `aws:SourceAccount`. Observed: the schedule-ARN condition was refused. The cause is not confirmed: Scheduler may send the schedule-group ARN instead (untried), and IAM propagation delay of a fresh role is a second candidate. If the next deploy fails the same way, retry once before changing the policy again; a group-scoped `aws:SourceArn` can be tried later to restore the stricter guard.

The owner decided to trust `scheduler.amazonaws.com` with the `aws:SourceAccount` condition only. The `aws:SourceArn` condition is removed from both roles; this replaces the earlier schedule-ARN confused-deputy guard. The exposure stays small: each role can only invoke the one nightly function or start the one table export, and the account condition remains. Permissions, names, tags and the boundary are unchanged. Tests pin the exact trust policy and assert that no `aws:SourceArn` is present.
