Overview

Cupmark is an iPhone app (iOS 17 or later, English and German) for people who care about the coffee they brew.

  • Scan the barcode of a coffee bag. Known beans fill in by themselves; unknown ones you describe from pick lists.
  • Brew with the built-in timer. Method, dose, grind and water are chosen from lists and steppers.
  • Rate on eight fixed criteria and a closed vocabulary of flavour notes, so every rating can be compared.
  • Repeat a recipe with one tap. Your history and your own statistics are free.
  • Works offline. Everything syncs when you are back online. You can export your data as CSV at any time.

Nobody types. There is no text field anywhere in the app: no name, no email, no reviews. That keeps entries comparable, and it means there is nothing to moderate and nothing personal to leak.

Private by default. Only aggregates are ever shown, never a single person's log, and only once at least five different people have rated a bean. Contributing to them is your choice and off until you agree to it.

Later, a paid tier will show what all structured brews say about a bean: its score profile, the method that suits it and the recipes that score highest.

What the app does not do

No advertising, no tracking, no analytics, no crash reporting and no third-party software development kits for any of these. The app asks for no name, no email address, no location and no contacts. Nothing is typed anywhere.

What is stored, and why

  • Your account. You sign in with Apple. The app asks only for a pseudonymous account identifier. Apple gives us no name and no email address. The identifier is kept, in an Amazon Cognito user record, to know which data is yours (contract, Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).
  • Your log. Beans on your shelf, brews, ratings, recipes and your settings (your language and your choice about contributing to averages), kept so the app works and syncs between your devices (Art. 6(1)(b)).
  • Label photos. If you photograph a bag label, the photo is sent to our server and may be checked by the operator to confirm a bean for the shared catalogue. It is never shown to other users, not even back to you. The server removes all metadata, including any location, and keeps only the cleaned copy. The original you uploaded still has its metadata until it is deleted automatically, within about two days.
  • Your choice about averages. Whether you contribute to community averages is stored with your profile. The default is no. Your brews count towards averages only if you said yes when you wrote them (consent, Art. 6(1)(a)); you can change this in the app at any time.
  • Bean look-ups. When you scan a barcode, our server looks the number up in the Open Food Facts database and may keep a copy of the product's name, brand and image in the shared catalogue. Open Food Facts data is available under the Open Database License (ODbL). The look-up is made by our server, not by your phone, so Open Food Facts does not see your device.
  • Technical logs. The servers keep the technical records that running a service needs (for example request errors) for two weeks, only to keep the service secure and working (Art. 6(1)(f)).

Community averages

Averages are shown only when at least five different people have rated a bean. No individual log, rating or profile is shown to anyone else. When an account is deleted, its brews are removed and subtracted from the averages.

Where it is processed

The data is stored with Amazon Web Services in Frankfurt, Germany (region eu-central-1). Amazon Web Services EMEA SARL acts as our processor. Sign in with Apple is provided by Apple, which handles the sign-in as a separate controller under its own privacy policy. We do not send app data to any other recipient, and we do not move app data out of the EU ourselves.

How long it is kept, and deleting it

Your data is kept while your account exists. In the app, Settings has a button to delete your account. It removes your sign-in account record, shelf entries, brews, ratings, recipes and profile, and every version of the label photos you uploaded, straight away. Photos of beans that were already verified stay in the shared catalogue for 90 days after the verification (up to about four months counting older versions), but they are no longer linked to you.

Backups: after a deletion, copies can remain in the database's point-in-time recovery for up to 35 days and in weekly exports for up to about 13 months. A deletion log is kept so that any restore from a backup removes deleted accounts again before the data is used. Each entry holds only the account identifier, the identifiers of its label photos and the time of deletion. Entries are deleted after 400 days (up to about 14 months in all, counting older versions). Short-lived technical records, such as daily usage counters and unfinished uploads, expire by themselves.

Your rights

You can ask for access to your data, correction, deletion, restriction, a copy in a common format and you can object to processing based on legitimate interests. The app already gives you a CSV export and account deletion. For anything else write to the contact above. You may withdraw consent at any time. You also have the right to complain to a data protection authority, in particular in the country where you live or work.

Camera

The app uses the camera only to read a barcode, which happens on your device, and, if you choose, to photograph a label. Only a label photo you choose to take is uploaded.
